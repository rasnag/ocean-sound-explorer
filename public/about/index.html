About Curiosity Loop

“I don’t know. Let’s find out.”

Curiosity Loop makes short games from the questions children ask. A parent saves a question when it comes up, and we turn it into something they can investigate together when they have time.

It started with my nephew and a giant squid

My nephew used to suggest we “ask Alexa” when we wanted to know something. I understood the appeal. I’d been a teacher before I worked on Alexa at Amazon, and I’d spent years helping people get answers more easily.

When he wanted to know about a giant squid, I made him a game. He could choose how deep to dive and what light to use to look for one. While he played, he started asking more questions about the animals he encountered. I wanted to give more children a chance to follow their questions that way.

Not knowing is what makes it a game

I worry about how quickly children can come to see being wrong as something to avoid. I want them to have a place where it feels good to say, “I don’t know. Let’s find out.”

In a Curiosity Loop game, the child and the grown-up are co-investigators. They each make a guess, try something, and talk about what surprised them. The grown-up might guess wrong first. Then both of them get to look at what happened, change one thing, and try again.

That can start with a question at bedtime and carry on the next day at the kitchen table. We try to give families something they can keep exploring away from the screen, especially when the game leaves them with another question.

How we use AI

AI helps draft games behind the scenes. Children never interact directly with AI in Curiosity Loop. A person reviews each game for accuracy, safety, and age appropriateness before a child plays.

I’m also careful about what I claim children learn. A child can finish a game and still be figuring out why something worked. I’m interested in what kids can explain in their own words, what makes them change their minds, and whether they can use an idea later when they face a different question.

Rasna, Chief Investigator at Curiosity Loop
